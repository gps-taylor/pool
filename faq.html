Timeline & process

How long does it take to build a pool?

About six to eight weeks for a concrete pool, from dig day to first swim. That’s not a misprint. Before the dig, a few things have to happen — financing (if you use it), HOA approval, utility marking and permits — and those usually take one to two weeks each. Your consultant will give you a timeline specific to your pool, and we’ll tell you right away if anything could delay it.

What are the steps?

Consultation → 3D design and bid (48 hours) → contract and $1,000 deposit → HOA approval → permits → construction (dig, forms and steel, plumbing, shotcrete, tile and plaster, decking) → fill and startup.

Do you handle permits and HOA approval?

Yes — all of it. We create the permit plan, prepare the drawings and documentation for your HOA, pull permits from the city or county, and schedule inspections in Las Vegas, Henderson, Clark County and beyond.

How much is the deposit?

Always $1,000. Never more.

How will I know what’s happening during construction?

You’ll have one project manager — usually Grant — plus a private client page with a progress tracker, jobsite photos, and your 3D designs.

Design & options

Can a concrete pool be any shape or size?

Yes. Concrete pools can be designed in any shape and size to fit how you live — from spools and cocktail pools to resort-style builds.

What features can I add?

Swim-up bars, vanishing edges, raised spas, tanning ledges, bench seating, waterfalls, real and faux rock, grottos, laminar jets, fire bowls, fiber-optic and LED lighting, automation, and in-floor cleaning.

My yard is small. Can I still have a pool?

Almost always. Small and hillside yards often inspire the best designs. Spools — spa-sized pools up to about 12×14 ft and around four feet deep — are made for them. Your design and estimate are free.

How does Clark County’s 600 sq ft rule affect me?

Clark County limits new residential pools to 600 square feet of water surface. Most Las Vegas pools are already under that, and we design to the limit from the first sketch.

Salt water or chlorine?

Salt costs more up front but saves on chemicals every year. The salt cell typically lasts 3–5 years and needs cleaning every 3–6 months. Most people find salt water silky, less irritating and free of chlorine smell. A salt chlorine generator is required.

Cost & financing

What does a pool cost?

Pool cost is driven mostly by water surface area and perimeter — shape matters much less. The biggest swing is hardscape: decks, patios, waterfalls, planters and outdoor living. Access, topography, demolition and permit fees also matter. Try our cost planner for a planning range; your exact price comes with your 3D design.

Why do quotes vary so much for pools that look the same?

Because the differences are hidden: steel, plumbing, equipment, finish quality, warranty and workmanship. Ask every builder exactly how the pool will be built and exactly what equipment is specified.

Is financing available?

Yes. We work with three lenders — Viking Capital, Lyon Financial and HFS — offering options from equity lines to no-equity personal loans. See the financing page. Interest may be tax-deductible; ask your tax professional.

Equipment & warranty

What equipment do you use?

Jandy, our partner for more than seven years. Jandy equipment carries a 3-year warranty, compared with the 1 year most competitors offer.

Do you offer in-floor cleaning?

Yes — the A&A in-floor system with pop-up cleaning heads that sweep the pool about every hour, improve circulation, and can cut heating costs by up to 50%. It carries a limited lifetime warranty.

Do you do remodels?

We do select remodels. Because we’re busy building new pools, we’ll sometimes recommend a trusted specialist when that saves you money. Call or text Adrian to talk it through.
